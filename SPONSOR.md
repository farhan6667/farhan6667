# Sponsor my open source work

I build free tools for security and system administration, and I work as a cyber security professional. Most of what I publish starts as something I needed and could not find. If these tools save you or your team time, there are a few ways to help keep them alive and growing.

- [Wazuh RuleGuard](https://github.com/farhan6667/wazuh-ruleguard): test Wazuh detection changes before they ship.
- [Wazuh NoiseLens](https://github.com/farhan6667/wazuh-noiselens): see which alerts an exception would hide before you add it.
- [no-api-media-mcp](https://github.com/farhan6667/no-api-media-mcp): make images and videos from your coding agent with the AI plans you already pay for.
- [SFA Monitor](https://github.com/farhan6667/sfa-monitor): a free desktop performance widget and task manager for Linux.

All four are free to use.

## What sponsorship helps with

Honestly, it is mostly the unglamorous parts:

- **Test infrastructure.** The biggest gap in the Wazuh tools is validation on real managers and real versions. That needs machines to run them on.
- **Hosting and CI.** Build minutes, container storage, and a home for docs and demos.
- **Time.** Reviewing issues and pull requests, answering questions, and writing the documentation that people actually read.

## Ways to sponsor

I do not have fixed price tiers. Tell me what you can offer and we will work out something that suits both sides.

**1. Hosting, cloud and CI.** Server or cloud credits, hosting for docs and demo sites, extra CI minutes. If you run a hosting or cloud company, this is the easiest and most useful way to help, and no money has to cross a border.

**2. Tools and licences.** Security tools, SIEM or EDR trial licences, lab hardware, or accounts that let me test the projects against more environments.

**3. Funding.** One time or recurring. GitHub Sponsors is not available in Pakistan, where I live, so I handle funding directly by email instead of through a button that would not work. Send me a short note and I will reply with the options.

**4. Paid work through NexaForge.** Security reviews, penetration testing, detection engineering and custom tooling. It is real work for a real fee, and it also keeps the open source side running. See [NexaForge](https://nexaforge.eu.cc/).

**5. Your time.** A real-world test result, a good bug report, a fix, or a clearer sentence in the docs is often worth more than money. Start with a [good first issue](https://github.com/farhan6667/wazuh-ruleguard/labels/good%20first%20issue).

## How paying works

I live in Pakistan, where the usual sponsor buttons (GitHub Sponsors, Buy Me a Coffee, Ko-fi) cannot pay out, so I do it by hand. It is simple:

1. **Write to me** at nexaforge.services@gmail.com. Say what you would like to offer and which project it is for.
2. **We agree what suits you.** That can be help in kind (hosting, cloud credits, licences, tools), a one-off or monthly amount, or paid work through NexaForge.
3. **For money, I send you the payment details.** Depending on the amount that is a secure payment link where you can pay by card or bank transfer without opening any account, or bank details for a wire transfer. Card payments cost more in fees than a bank transfer, so for larger amounts a transfer is kinder to both of us.
4. **I confirm, say thank you, and add your name** (if you want it) to the sponsors list.

If you are in Pakistan, a local bank transfer is the easiest and cheapest.

## What sponsors get

- Your name or logo and a link in the README of the projects you support, if you want it.
- A thank you in the release notes.
- A direct line to me for questions about the tools, and your feedback counts when I plan what to build next.

What you do not get: control of the project, hidden changes, or any say in what the tools report. They stay open source and independent. I will not take sponsorship that asks me to hide a vulnerability or to change how a tool reports security results.
